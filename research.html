Research

My work joins three threads: extracting signal from seismic noise, building images of the subsurface, and using machine learning to make both faster and more reliable. The figures below are live, simplified versions of those methods.

Ambient noise interferometry

Ocean waves, wind and traffic make the ground vibrate all the time. Cross-correlating that noise between two stations, then stacking day after day, makes random fluctuations cancel while the coherent wave travelling between the stations grows.

The result approximates the Green's function, as if one station were a source and the other a receiver.

Seismic tomography

Travel times between many station pairs are inverted for a 3-D shear-velocity model. Red regions are slower than average, often warmer, fluid-rich or sedimentary rock; blue regions are faster, usually colder or older lithosphere.

Time series analysis

Before any imaging, continuous records are cleaned and scanned for arrivals. Filtering removes long-period drift and high-frequency noise; a short-term over long-term average ratio (STA/LTA) flags sudden energy, giving a first-arrival pick.

Deep learning for seismology

Classic triggers struggle with noisy data and overlapping events. A neural network trained on labelled waveforms reads each window and outputs the probability of a P arrival, an S arrival or noise, scaling to years of continuous data.
